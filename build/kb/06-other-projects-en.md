## What did Santiago build at the San Francisco Build Day? Praise Gap
On September 19, 2026, Santiago Llaguno took part in the Claude Build Day in San Francisco, built around Claude Fable 5.1. At that Build Day, Santiago built "Praise Gap" on his own. Praise Gap is an agent for small businesses: you give it a business and its city, and within a few minutes it tells you what customers praise about that business's closest competitors on Google Maps that nobody says about the business itself. Every claim Praise Gap makes quotes a review word for word.
Source: ledger P8

## How does Praise Gap avoid making things up? A second context that refutes and a script that checks quotes
In Praise Gap, the agent Santiago Llaguno built at the San Francisco Build Day, a second context of the same model tries to knock down every claim. That second context never saw how the claims were written. Then a 40-line script checks that every quote appears word for word in the evidence. Anything that fails either check is dropped and stays in a visible log.
Source: ledger P8

## What are Praise Gap's limits? Public data only and a low cost per business
Praise Gap, Santiago Llaguno's agent from the San Francisco Build Day, works only with public data. Praise Gap uses no financial data and no personal data about the people who write the reviews. The scraping for Praise Gap costs around 30 US cents per business.
Source: ledger P8

## What's next for Praise Gap? Running it as a product on Managed Agents
As the next step for Praise Gap, Santiago Llaguno designed how to run it as a product on Anthropic's Managed Agents. That design includes a rubric, a sandbox, a budget per analysis and scheduled monthly reports.
Source: ledger P8

## How does Santiago design evals? Reusable sets by criteria
Santiago Llaguno reuses eval sets across his projects and adapts them to each new project. Santiago designs his evals around these criteria: language, the client's business goals, how fluent and natural a role play feels, conversation scoring, and cognitive load.
Source: ledger P9
